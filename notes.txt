 QAOA--

 
Step 4: build the QAOA ansatz circuit for the Z2 LGT ground-state search.

    |psi(beta, gamma)> = prod_{i=1}^{p} exp(-i*beta_i*H_E) exp(-i*gamma_i*H_B)
                          |+>^{\\otimes n}

Design choices, matching the paper's "shallow circuit, single- and two-qubit
gates only" philosophy:

  * Initial state |+>^n = ground state of H_E. One Hadamard per qubit.

  * Mixer layer  exp(-i*beta*H_E):
        H_E = sum_l (1 - X_l)  is a sum of COMMUTING single-qubit terms, so
        exp(-i*beta*H_E) = (global phase) * prod_l RX_l(-2*beta)
        -> one single-qubit RX gate per qubit. No entangling gates at all.

  * Problem layer  exp(-i*gamma*H_B):
        H_B = -sum_p Z_{p1}Z_{p2}Z_{p3}Z_{p4}  is a sum of 4-body ZZZZ terms,
        one per plaquette. Each term is implemented as a standard
        "CNOT staircase": CNOT chain into an ancilla-free target qubit,
        one Rz, then CNOT chain back out. That's 6 CNOTs + 1 Rz per
        plaquette, using only 1- and 2-qubit gates -- directly hardware
        compatible. We let Qiskit's PauliEvolutionGate do this compilation
        (it produces exactly this staircase for a Trotter step of a single
        Pauli string), and we also show the decomposed circuit once so you
        can see the actual native gates.

We build the full parametrized circuit as a function of (betas, gammas) with
p layers, each stored as qiskit Parameters so we can hand it to a classical
optimizer later (step 5).

OPTIMIze - 

============

Step 5: classical optimization loop that drives the QAOA parameters
(beta, gamma) to minimize <psi(beta,gamma)| H(h) |psi(beta,gamma)>.

Pipeline for one energy evaluation:
    1. bind numeric (beta, gamma) values into the parametrized circuit
    2. get the exact statevector via qiskit.quantum_info.Statevector
       (noiseless simulation -- fine for our tiny 12-qubit test lattice;
       for larger systems you'd swap this for qiskit-aer's shot-based or
       matrix-product-state simulator, or actual hardware)
    3. compute the expectation value of H as a SparsePauliOp directly
       against the statevector (exact, no sampling noise)
    4. hand the energy to scipy.optimize.minimize (COBYLA: derivative-free,
       appropriate here since we're not providing analytic gradients)

We also do multi-start optimization: QAOA energy landscapes are riddled with
local minima, so we try several random initializations and keep the best.

Physical Basis
------------------------------------

-apply a Hadamard gate to every single qubit at once. Hadamard has a special property — it swaps X and Z
-The rule A_v = product of X's around vertex v becomes A_v' = product of Z's around vertex v.
-A product-of-Z's operator has a very simple, direct meaning on an ordinary computational-basis bitstring:
   its eigenvalue is just +1 or −1 depending on whether an even or odd number of the "1" bits in the string sit on links touching v
-A bitstring where every vertex is touched an even number of times by "1" bits is, in graph theory, called a cycle (think of the "1" bits
   as edges you've drawn in; a cycle is exactly the way you can trace closed loops through a graph without ever leaving a vertex by an odd
   number of unmatched paths). The set of all such bitstrings is called the graph's cycle space, and it's a vector space of a known, exact
   size: 2^k, where k = (number of edges) − (number of vertices) + 1. For our 18-qubit, 9-vertex lattice: k = 18 − 9 + 1 = 10. So there 
   are exactly 1024 legal bitstrings, not 262,144.

---PROVED VIA HADAMARD DUALITY---Ground State will be between these 1024 states

Step 11: reproduce the paper's actual confinement evidence -- fitting

    <W_Gamma> = exp(-chi(h)*A_Gamma - delta(h)*P_Gamma)

by computing MULTIPLE Wilson loop sizes on MULTIPLE lattice sizes (the
paper studies L=3,4,5) and doing a linear least-squares fit of
ln|<W>| = -chi*A - delta*P.

Honesty check first: can we actually run this at L=4, L=5 the way we ran
QAOA at L=3? NO -- L=4 needs a 2^32-element statevector (68.7 GB), L=5
needs 2^50 (astronomically more). This sandbox has ~7.3 GB of RAM. Real
QAOA circuit simulation is a hard wall past L=3 here; the paper's own
L=4,5 results either used a bigger machine, a smarter simulator (tensor
networks), or real hardware.

What we CAN still do honestly: the reduced (cycle-space) exact
diagonalization from physical_basis.py doesn't need the full 2^n space --
its dimension is 2^k with k = n_links - n_vertices + 1, which is far
smaller (2^17 = 131,072 at L=4 -- trivial; 2^26 = 67,108,864 at L=5 --
likely too big for THIS sandbox too, see the end of this file). So we get
genuine EXACT ground-truth physics (not QAOA-approximated) at L=3 and
L=4, computed classically, and we're upfront if/when L=5 also fails.

Wilson loop operator in the reduced (Hadamard-dual) frame
------------------------------------------------------------
Recall from physical_basis.py: in the transformed frame, Z_l -> X_l, so
the Wilson loop W_Gamma = prod_{l in Gamma} Z_l becomes, in that frame,
prod_{l in Gamma} X_l -- an operator that FLIPS every bit along the loop.
Since a simple closed loop touches every vertex an even number of times,
its bitmask is itself in the cycle space, so flipping it keeps you inside
the physical subspace -- we can compute <W_Gamma> directly from the
reduced ground-state vector, without ever forming the ambient 2^n state:

    <psi| W_Gamma |psi> = sum_j psi[index(x_j XOR mask_Gamma)] * psi[j]

Building a loop as a plaquette union
--------------------------------------
Any *contractible* (non-wraparound) rectangular a x b Wilson loop can be
built by XOR-ing together the a*b elementary plaquette masks that tile it
-- shared interior edges appear in two plaquettes and cancel out via XOR,
leaving exactly the outer boundary. Much simpler than tracing a path by
hand, and it's exactly the kind of trick this Hadamard/cycle-space
representation makes natural.



Step 12: the four degenerate topological ground states on the torus,
labeled |tau_h, tau_v> = |++>, |+->, |-+>, |-->.

't Hooft loop operators (from the paper, confirmed by fetching the exact
definition rather than guessing):

    tau_C = prod_{l cap C} sigma^x_l

where C is a closed path in the DUAL lattice, and "l cap C" means the
links of the ORIGINAL lattice that C crosses transversally. On our square
lattice, a straight dual-lattice line running (say) horizontally through
"row gap" r0 crosses every VERTICAL link in that row -- one full ring of
L links wrapping around the torus. A straight dual line running through
"column gap" c0 crosses every HORIZONTAL link in that column -- the other
wrapping ring. These are the two INEQUIVALENT non-contractible loops on a
torus (you can't shrink either to a point, and you can't deform one into
the other), which is exactly why there are two independent tau operators
tau_h, tau_v rather than just one.

Why 4 sectors, and how we find them without new machinery
-------------------------------------------------------------
tau_h, tau_v are built entirely from X operators, so (like the star
operators A_v) they become DIAGONAL under the same Hadamard transform we
used for physical_basis.py's reduced exact diagonalization: in that
transformed frame, X_l -> Z_l, so tau_h', tau_v' are diagonal with
eigenvalue (-1)^(parity of the loop's bits in the bitstring x'). That
means we don't need any new representation -- we can just split the
physical basis states we already enumerated in physical_basis.py into 4
groups by their (tau_h, tau_v) parity, and diagonalize H within each
group separately. Since [tau_h, H] = [tau_v, H] = 0 (checked explicitly
below, not just assumed), each group is an invariant subspace: energies
computed this way are exact, and the four numbers we get ARE the four
sectors' exact ground energies.
